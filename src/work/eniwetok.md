The essay examines Eniwetok Atoll, the site of numerous US nuclear tests, and the paradox it poses for cultural heritage: a place of ecological richness and Indigenous significance turned into a symbol of technological destruction. Drawing on Chakrabarty, Meskell's "negative heritage", Lowenthal and Latour, it argues that heritage in the Anthropocene has to hold the scars of the recent past — the Runit Dome, displacement, contamination — alongside its achievements.

At its centre is the account of my grandfather, Bun O'Shea, who saw the aftermath of the tests:

> "More than 10 years ago, I walked onto the blood-stained coral island called Eniwetok. I used to swim under water with water-tight glasses on to better observe the coral formations, the sea vegetation, and the beautifully colored and oddly-shaped fish that would swim by my eyes. I was thinking of all this while looking at pictures of the H-bomb blast. The island I describe is now only a black crater in the sea."
